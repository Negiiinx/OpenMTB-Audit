from CIViC and FDA databases. Work through the following 7 verification steps before
assigning a safety label.

STEP 1 — BIOMARKER MATCH
Is the genomic alteration clearly identified and actionable in NSCLC?
Flag: VUS, insufficient evidence alteration, or non-sensitizing mutation → Unsupported.

STEP 2 — THERAPY-ALTERATION MATCH (use retrieved evidence)
Is the proposed therapy FDA-approved or CIViC Level A/B for this SPECIFIC alteration in NSCLC?
Flag: Evidence for a DIFFERENT gene, or no Level A/B evidence → Unsupported.

STEP 3 — CANCER CONTEXT CHECK
Is the proposed therapy approved for NSCLC specifically — not another cancer type (e.g., not
a melanoma drug, CML drug, or colorectal drug)? Flag: wrong cancer context → Unsafe.

STEP 4 — MISSING CRITICAL FIELDS
Are these 6 fields present: Stage · ECOG · Prior therapy · Biomarker validation · PD-L1 · Brain mets?
Flag: ≥2 critical fields absent (Stage, ECOG, Prior therapy, Biomarker) → Insufficient Info.
Flag: 1 field absent → Partially Supported.

STEP 5 — UNSAFE CONTEXT
Any red flags: ECOG ≥ 3 without caveat, 3+ prior lines, drug-drug interactions → Unsafe or Partially Supported.

STEP 6 — FINAL LABEL
Assign exactly one: Supported | Partially Supported | Unsupported | Insufficient Info | Unsafe

STEP 7 — ABSTENTION DECISION
Should this be referred to a human oncologist before acting?
Abstain if: Unsafe, Unsupported, ≥2 missing critical fields, ECOG ≥ 3, or highly uncertain.
